# User Interviews

## Research Method

Conducted informal conversations with 8 Myntra / online fashion shoppers
from my friends and family network to understand how they use product
reviews while purchasing fashion products online.

The conversations focused on existing user behaviour rather than testing
or promoting a specific solution.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Reviews are an important part of the purchase decision

All interviewed users said that they check product reviews before
purchasing fashion products online.

Reviews act as an additional layer of confidence beyond the product
description and images.

---

### 2. Users primarily look for "real product" information

The information users care about most is not just the overall rating.

They specifically look for signals around:

- Fit / sizing
- Fabric quality
- Overall product quality
- Whether the actual product matches expectations
- How the product looks or feels in real life

This indicates that users use reviews to answer a question that the
standard product page cannot completely answer:

> "What will this product actually be like when I receive it?"

---

### 3. Product expectation mismatches are common

Users shared previous experiences where the product they received did
not completely match their expectations.

Examples included:

- Ill-fitting clothes
- Fabric quality being different from what they expected
- The product looking different from what they imagined from the listing

This suggests that the purchase decision contains an element of
uncertainty even when product information is already available.

---

### 4. Users do not have the patience to process hundreds of reviews

Although users value reviews, they generally do not read every review
when a product has a large number of them.

Instead, users tend to:

- Check the most recent reviews
- Scan reviews selectively
- Look for specific information they care about

This creates an important information problem:

> The information users need may already exist, but finding the
> relevant signal requires manual effort.

---

### 5. Ratings alone do not explain the customer experience

A product can have a high overall rating while individual reviews
contain recurring concerns about specific aspects such as fit, fabric,
colour or quality.

Therefore, the problem is not simply a lack of reviews.

The problem is **extracting meaningful patterns from the reviews that
already exist.**

---

# Key Insights

### Insight 1 — Reviews contain high-value product intelligence

Customer reviews are more than a feedback mechanism.

They contain real-world information about what happened after the
customer purchased and experienced the product.

---

### Insight 2 — The valuable information is buried in unstructured feedback

The information users care about is distributed across individual
review texts, photos and experiences.

Users have to manually scan this information to identify recurring
patterns.

---

### Insight 3 — There is an information-to-insight gap

Myntra already provides customers with a large amount of product
information.

However:

**Information available ≠ Information easily understood**

The gap is between:

Product information + hundreds of reviews
                ↓
        User understanding

The user currently has to perform much of this interpretation manually.

---

### Core Product Insight

> **The problem is not that shoppers lack reviews.  
> The problem is that the most useful information inside those reviews
> is difficult to discover quickly.**

This creates an opportunity to transform unstructured customer feedback
into a concise, actionable understanding of the product's
real-world experience.
